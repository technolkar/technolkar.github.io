About

A mechanical engineer who moved into embedded systems by building things people actually used.

I started in a polytechnic workshop in Belagavi, India, wiring servo controllers into a humanoid robot with eight classmates. It won second prize at a national technical event, and it set the pattern for everything since: learn the hardware by building the hardware.

After my bachelor's in mechanical engineering I founded Technolkar Innovations, making do-it-yourself electronics kits for school and engineering students. I ran the whole loop myself — circuit design in Proteus, firmware in the Arduino IDE, testing, soldering, assembly, packaging, documentation, the YouTube channel that marketed it, and the workshops that taught students how to use the kits. Fifty-plus kits and two hundred-plus trained students later, I paused it: the market for that product was not there, and I wanted the theory behind what I had been building by instinct.

So I came to Germany. At THD I work on cyber-physical systems — most recently leading a seven-person team building a VR driver-perspective simulation in Unity with AI pedestrian behaviour and hardware-in-the-loop steering, plus AR/VR and additive-manufacturing case studies and parametric gear design in Lua.

My interest sits where mechanical design, embedded firmware and real-time control meet: robots that have to work in an actual room, not just in simulation. I am looking for a master's thesis placement or internship in robotics or embedded systems, in or around Munich.

Projects

THD Germany · 2024–25
The pedestrian system, running

Driver-Perspective Active Safety System

A four-level VR driving trainer built in Unity — day, night, fog and rain conditions, autonomous emergency braking and ABS, crash detection with logged reaction times, and an inverse-kinematics driver whose hands track the wheel. It runs on a Meta Quest 3 with a HORI racing wheel and pedals. Seven-person team at THD; I led it.

My part was the pedestrian and traffic system. Mixamo characters rigged with NavMesh agents, footpaths baked as the only walkable surface, an AI spawner that clones pedestrians across the whole city, and a wander script that sends each one to random footpath destinations — so the city fills with people who distract the driver without ever stepping into the road. Traffic vehicles follow waypoint routes on the same principle.

Unity 3D · C# · NavMesh · Mixamo · Meta Quest 3 · HORI wheel
